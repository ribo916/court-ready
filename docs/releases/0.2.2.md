# Release 0.2.2

Makes the backup actually work on the only device the app runs on.

## The problem

Court Ready is installed as a home-screen app on iPhone, and that is the sole copy of the data. The 0.2 export built a hidden `<a download>` and clicked it. That is fine on desktop and unreliable inside an installed iOS app, where standalone mode frequently swallows the download. The safety net for the entire product could quietly do nothing on the one device that matters.

The implementation also revoked the blob URL immediately after `click()`, which can cancel a download before the blob has been read.

## Changes

- **Share sheet first.** Export builds a `File` and offers it through `navigator.share` when the platform can share files, which is the dependable route to Files or iCloud Drive from an installed iOS app. Cancelling the sheet is treated as a no-op, not an error.
- **Download as the fallback**, for desktop and anywhere sharing is unavailable. The object URL is now revoked after a delay instead of immediately.
- **Copy as a last resort.** Puts the whole backup on the clipboard to paste into Notes or an email, so there is still a route out if both other paths fail.
- **Permissive file picker.** Restore previously filtered on `application/json`; iOS maps JSON to inconsistent UTIs and could grey out the very file the app had just written. The filter now also accepts `.json` and `text/plain`.
- **Backup reminders.** The card asks for a backup once three days of history exist with no export ever recorded, and every 14 days after an export. `lastExportedAt` is stored in `court-ready:v2:meta`.
- **Honest copy.** The card now names the real risks — deleting the app from the home screen and clearing website data — rather than a vague warning about eviction.

## Correction to earlier guidance

Previous notes overstated the eviction risk. Apple's seven-day cap on script-writable storage applies to Safari browsing; home-screen web apps keep their own usage counter, so daily use protects the data from that rule. The real hazards are deleting the app and clearing website data, both instant and unrecoverable.

## Testing

- 89 unit tests, up from 81. New coverage for the meta record, the day count, and the nudge thresholds on both sides of the boundary.
- Browser-verified: the share path with a stubbed `navigator.share` (payload, filename, and check-in round-trip), the download fallback with sharing removed, the clipboard fallback, and the nudge appearing and going quiet.

**Not verified:** the real iOS share sheet. That needs a physical device. The branch it takes is tested; the native sheet it hands off to is not.
